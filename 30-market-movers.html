30 Market Movers

Understanding What Drives Stock Prices — from a single headline to the entire global economy

THE BIG IDEA

No Single Thing Moves a Stock

THE KID VERSION "A stock's price is like the weather. Sunshine (good news), wind (interest rates), storms (world events), and even the mood of the people outside (investor sentiment) all mix together to decide what actually happens. You can't predict the weather from just ONE thing — and you can't predict a stock's price from just one thing either. This module walks through the 30 biggest ingredients in that mix, grouped into 6 categories."

Every factor below is real and documented — but they rarely act alone. The skill isn't memorizing 30 rules, it's recognizing which ones are actively pulling on a stock right now, and whether they're pulling the same direction or fighting each other.

CATEGORY 1 OF 6

🏢 Company-Specific Factors

These come directly from the company itself — its news, numbers, products, people, and decisions.

Company News

A single headline can move price within minutes. Positive catalysts: +5-15% in a session. Negative events: -10-30%. Impact fades over time as the news gets "priced in."

Earnings Reports

The quarterly "judgment day." Watch EPS vs. estimates, revenue growth, margins, and forward guidance. The unofficial "whisper number" often matters more than the official estimate.

Dividends

New dividend: +2-5%. Dividend increase: +1-3%. Dividend cut: -5-15% — cuts are read as a red flag on financial health.

Products

Launches and recalls swing price hard. Apple +8.5% on the original iPhone reveal; Samsung lost $17B in the Note 7 battery crisis; Peloton -35% on treadmill recalls.

Layoffs

Counterintuitively often +2-5% — investors like lower costs. Context matters: company-specific cuts during a growth phase read as a red flag.

CEO / Leadership Activity

Planned successions: minimal disruption. Unexpected exits: 5-10% volatility. Executive misconduct: -10-25% with lasting reputational damage.

Insider Transactions

Executives buying their own stock: +1-3% confidence signal. Multiple executives selling at once often precedes bad news.

Share Buybacks

Reduces the number of shares in circulation — all else equal, that scarcity alone can lift the price per share.

Mergers & Acquisitions

Target company: +20-50% on announcement. Acquiring company: often -2-5% as the market questions overpayment. Failed deals can crash back to pre-announcement levels.

CATEGORY 2 OF 6

🌍 Macro Economic Forces

These live above any single company — they shift the entire market's floor and ceiling at once.

Interest Rates

"The economy's thermostat." Hikes pressure growth stocks, help financials. Cuts help dividend stocks and real estate. Bond yields compete directly with stocks for investor capital.

Inflation

"The silent wealth eroder." Squeezes margins for companies that can't pass costs to customers. Central banks fight it with rate hikes. Hard assets (commodities, real estate) often do better.

Deflation

"The demand destroyer." Consumers delay purchases waiting for lower prices; debt burdens grow in real terms. Japan's "Lost Decades" is the textbook cautionary example.

GDP

Above-forecast growth boosts cyclical stocks; below-forecast helps defensive sectors. Two straight quarters of negative GDP has historically correlated with 20-30% market declines.

Unemployment

Rising unemployment pressures consumer discretionary stocks. Falling unemployment tends to benefit retailers and financials. Wage growth data hints at future inflation.

Exchange Rates

A strong dollar shrinks the reported value of overseas earnings; a weak dollar boosts them. Roughly 40% of S&P 500 revenue comes from international operations.

CATEGORY 3 OF 6

⚡ World Events & Shocks

Unpredictable, high-impact, and often global in reach.

Geopolitical Events

Initial shock: -1-5%. Capital flees to safe havens (Treasuries, gold, Swiss franc, yen). VIX often spikes 20-40%. Markets typically recover within 1-3 months if fundamentals aren't actually hit.

Political Climate

Policy shifts, elections, and international relations changes ripple through regulation and investor confidence sector by sector.

Natural & Man-Made Disasters

Disasters near major economic hubs spook markets over feared broader economic damage — even when the direct financial exposure is limited.

Investor Sentiment

Tracked via the VIX, put/call ratios, and surveys like AAII's bull/bear spread. Extreme readings often signal a turning point is close.

Midday Trading Lull

Volume drops 11:30am-1:30pm ET as traders take a break. Opening (9:30-10:30) and closing (3-4pm) hours carry the heaviest volume — moves during the lull often reverse once volume returns.

Friday Trading Effect

Selling pressure builds Friday afternoon as traders de-risk before the weekend. Monday gaps often reflect weekend news. Monthly options expiration Fridays add extra volatility.

Trends

A stock moving up can gather its own momentum — "success breeds success" as more buyers pile into what's already working.

Liquidity

How easily a stock trades hands. Highly liquid stocks react fast and cleanly to news; illiquid stocks can gap violently on the same news.

Volatility

How much and how fast price swings. Higher volatility means bigger risk — and bigger potential reward.

Demand & Supply

The most basic force underneath everything else: more buyers than sellers pushes price up, and vice versa.

CATEGORY 5 OF 6

📊 Analyst & Institutional Influence

Wall Street's own opinions become a market-moving force in themselves.

Analyst Upgrades

+2-5% typical move; multiple upgrades compound the effect.

Analyst Downgrades

-3-7%, worse when paired with cut earnings estimates.

Price Target Changes

Even without a rating change, a target revision alone can move price 1-3%.

New Coverage Initiation

+1-4% just from the added visibility and trading volume, regardless of the actual rating.

Industry Performance

Sector rotation moves capital between industries. Leading stocks act as bellwethers; commodity-dependent industries (energy, airlines) move with input costs.

CATEGORY 6 OF 6

🌐 Broader Market Conditions

The overall backdrop everything else is trading against.

Retail Sales

A direct read on consumer health. A sustained drop-off signals a slowing economy; an upswing is read as bullish.

Market Conditions

The overall bull/bear backdrop shapes investor confidence — and can be broken down further by sector and industry.

Hype

Advertising, PR, and social buzz can inflate a stock beyond its fundamentals. Often the most volatile and least durable driver on this whole list.

🌈 Rainbow Engine rule: hype-driven moves are the ones most likely to fully reverse. If a rally has no earnings, no structural news, and no macro support underneath it — treat it as fragile.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

Mastering Market Dynamics

Multifactor Analysis

No single factor determines price. Integrate company fundamentals, economic indicators, and sentiment into one read.

Time Horizon Matters

Short-term moves often reflect news and sentiment; long-term performance aligns more with real fundamentals.

Interconnected Influences

These factors feed each other — interest rates affect currencies, which affect earnings, which affect sentiment, which drives trends.

Information Advantage

Your edge comes from the speed and quality of how you process these signals, not from knowing any single one.
