Calls & Puts — How They Work

The Two Scenarios Every Options Trade Is Built On

SCENARIO 1

The Call Option Scenario

THE KID VERSION "A call is like reserving your favorite toy at today's price because you think it's about to get more popular (more expensive). If you're right and it gets more expensive, your reservation is now worth a lot. If you're wrong, you only lose what you paid to reserve it."

📈 Call Option: the right to BUY an asset at a specified price

1. The buyer pays a premium for the right to buy the stock at the strike price.

2. Profit potential opens up when the stock price rises ABOVE the strike price.

3. The higher the stock climbs past the strike, the more the call option is worth.

SCENARIO 2

The Put Option Scenario

THE KID VERSION "A put is like buying insurance on something you think is about to drop in value. If it does drop, your insurance pays out. If it doesn't drop, you just lose the small cost of the insurance policy."

📉 Put Option: the right to SELL an asset at a specified price

1. The buyer pays a premium for the right to sell the stock at the strike price.

2. Profit potential opens up when the stock price falls BELOW the strike price.

3. The lower the stock drops past the strike, the more the put option is worth.

NEW VOCABULARY

In the Money, At the Money, Out of the Money

Where the stock price sits relative to the strike price has a name — this is called "moneyness," and it tells you instantly whether an option has real value right now or is still just a bet.

TermFor a CALLFor a PUT
In the Money (ITM)Stock price is ABOVE the strikeStock price is BELOW the strike
At the Money (ATM)Stock price ≈ the strikeStock price ≈ the strike
Out of the Money (OTM)Stock price is BELOW the strikeStock price is ABOVE the strike
🌈 Rainbow Engine rule: an option that's Out of the Money at expiration is worthless. The buyer's max loss is always just the premium paid — never more.
RISK PICTURE FOR BUYERS

Max Loss, Max Gain — Know Both Before You Click Buy

Buying a Call

Max loss: the premium paid (100%). Max gain: theoretically unlimited — the stock can keep climbing.

Buying a Put

Max loss: the premium paid (100%). Max gain: large but capped — a stock can only fall to $0.
