Opens the library to add RSI, MACD, HMA, DMI, and the rest of the 7-indicator toolkit to the chart.

f(x)

Custom formula/function builder for advanced calculated series.

1x1 Layout

Switch between single-chart and multi-chart grid layouts.

Extended Hrs / Real-Time

Toggle pre/post-market data and live vs. delayed pricing.

Templates

Save your full indicator stack as a reusable template — set it up once, apply it to any symbol.

Notes / My Charts / Alerts / Watch

Annotate a setup, save chart layouts, set price alerts, and add symbols to a watchlist.

THE EXTRA TOOLS

The Moving Average Stack & Volatility Stops

Five more tools that show up directly on the candles alongside the 7 core indicators. These build the actual overlay lines you see stacked on the chart — worth understanding in depth, not just recognizing by color.

TEMA 9 — Triple EMA, 9-Period

A moving average built from three layered EMAs, engineered to cut lag to a minimum. It reacts to price almost instantly — great for scalping entries, but it can flag a lot of small, noisy wiggles as "moves" too.

EMA 13 — The Calmer Fast Line

One notch slower than TEMA 9. When TEMA 9 crosses above/below EMA 13, that's an early trend-shift signal. In a trend, price pulling back to EMA 13 is a common re-entry zone.

EMA 48 — The Intraday Referee

The bigger-picture trend filter for the session. Price above EMA 48 = buyers hold the intraday edge; below it = sellers do. EMA 13 crossing EMA 48 confirms a real trend change, not just noise.

MA 20 — The Steady Baseline

A plain (unweighted) moving average — smoother and slower to react than any EMA. Acts as "normal" for price; when price stretches far away from MA 20, that's often when a snap-back becomes likely.

Volatility Stops — The Adaptive Leash

Built from recent volatility (ATR-style), this plots a trailing line that widens when the market is choppy and tightens when it's calm — so you're not stopped out early by normal noise, but you also don't give back excess profit when things settle down. When the line flips from one side of price to the other, that's both a trend-reversal warning AND the actual level your stop should be sitting at. This is a risk-management tool as much as an entry signal.

THE KID VERSION "TEMA 9 is the friend who texts back instantly — maybe too fast. EMA 13 waits half a second longer, so it's not as jumpy. EMA 48 is the referee for the whole day — which team is winning right now? MA 20 is the class average over the last 20 quizzes — your definition of 'normal.' And Volatility Stops is a leash: it gets longer when the dog (price) is running wild, and shorter when it's calm, so you don't yank it back too early or let it run too far."
🌈 Rainbow Engine rule: TEMA 9, EMA 13, and EMA 48 stacked together show trend at three speeds at once. When all three line up in the same order (fast above medium above slow, in an uptrend) that's a much stronger trend confirmation than any one line alone.
DO THIS YOURSELF

Setting Up Your Own Chart

1
Search your symbol (NQ, MNQ, SPY, TSLA — anything) in the top toolbar.
2
Set your timeframe to match the session — 1m for timing, 5m/15m for structure, matching Module 3's zoom-out method.
3
Open Indicators and add your core set: an HMA-style moving average, RSI or Stochastic RSI, and MACD or DMI.
4
Use the drawing tools to mark your support and resistance zones directly on the chart.
5
Save the whole layout as a Template so every new symbol you open starts with your full stack already applied.
6
If you're trading options on this symbol, jump to the OPTIONS section in the sidebar for the chain, Greeks, and expected move — no new platform needed.
