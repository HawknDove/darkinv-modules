What Is an Option?

Options Trading 101 · Trade with Kawanna — Contracts, Calls & Puts

MEET THE IDEA

An Option Is a Contract Between Two People

An options contract is an agreement between two parties around a stock, agreeing on a specific price (the strike price) that trade can happen at, any time before a set deadline (the expiration date).

THE KID VERSION "Imagine you find a toy you might want to buy in a month, but you're not sure yet. You pay the store $2 today to LOCK IN today's price for 30 days. If the toy gets more popular and pricier, you still get to buy it at the old price — you just paid $2 for that right. If you change your mind, you only lose the $2, not the full price of the toy."

That small $2 payment is called the premium — it's the price of the option contract itself, separate from the price of the stock underneath it.

THE TWO FLAVORS

Calls vs. Puts

📈 CALL

The right to BUY a stock at the strike price. You buy calls when you think the stock is going UP — it's a leveraged bet on appreciation.

📉 PUT

The right to SELL a stock at the strike price. You buy puts when you think the stock is going DOWN — it profits from a price decline.

🌈 Rainbow Engine rule: for stock options, ONE contract always covers 100 shares of the underlying stock — that's the multiplier behind every premium calculation.
WHY TRADERS USE OPTIONS

Leverage — Small Money, Big Exposure

Buying 100 shares of a $450 stock costs $45,000. Buying ONE call option that controls those same 100 shares might cost a few hundred dollars in premium. That's leverage: options let you control a large position for a small upfront cost — which magnifies both potential gains AND potential losses.

THE KID VERSION "It's like paying a small deposit to reserve a bike instead of buying the whole bike. If the bike becomes more valuable, your small deposit is now worth way more than what you paid. But if nobody wants the bike anymore, your deposit might be worth nothing — even though it was never the full price of the bike."
